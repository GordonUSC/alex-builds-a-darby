The Questions You Have Right Now

Q1What exactly IS a Darby?

Darby is Gordon's AI teammate: a Claude-powered agent that lives on a Mac Mini in his house. It reads the email he tells it to read, drafts in his voice, texts on his behalf with hard guardrails, remembers his people, builds him websites and dossiers (like this one, hello), and runs on a schedule even when he is asleep.

The engine underneath is OpenClaw, an open agent framework that connects a model (Claude) to channels (iMessage, WhatsApp, Discord, email) with a permission system in between. Your Darby will not be Gordon's Darby. It will be yours: your voice, your people, your rules, your name for it.

Q2What do I need before I start?
  • Your Mac Mini, signed into your own Apple ID (this is what lets it send iMessages as you, later, with your permission gates).
  • A Claude account (Anthropic). The onboarding step will walk you through connecting it; a paid plan is what makes a daily-driver agent practical.
  • An hour, unrushed. Forty minutes if you type like you talk.
  • A name. Gordon has Darby. You need your own. Do not skip this; naming it is the moment it becomes real.
Q3Is this going to text people without my permission?

No, and this is the most important design idea Gordon can hand you: the whitelist comes first. Out of the box your agent should talk to exactly one person: you. Every person it can message gets added by you, on purpose, one at a time. Gordon's Darby runs identity checks before every single send (right person, right thread, right channel) because one early mix-up between two friends taught the lesson permanently. Build that habit on day one and you will never learn it the hard way.

Q4Why is Gordon giving me this?

Because you asked good questions about Darby all year, because CYSTEM Year 4 runs better with a force multiplier behind its lead, and because the whole point of Good Vibes Only and GGP is that the people we love should be early to powerful tools, not late. Welcome. You are exactly on time.

The Build

Eight steps. Check them off; this page remembers your progress on this browser. Copy buttons on every command.

1 · Install Homebrew and Node 22
Open Terminal (Cmd+Space, type Terminal) and paste: 
/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"
brew install node@22
Homebrew is the Mac's package manager; Node is the runtime OpenClaw runs on (Gordon's stack runs node@22 exactly).
2 · Install OpenClaw
npm install -g openclaw
openclaw --version
If the version prints, you are in. (Gordon's Darby runs OpenClaw 2026.7.x.) Full install options live at docs.openclaw.ai/install if npm gives you attitude.
3 · Run the guided setup
openclaw onboard
This is the whole ballgame: it walks you through connecting your Claude account, choosing models, starting the Gateway (the always-on brain stem), creating your agent's workspace, and connecting channels. Take it slow, read each screen, answer honestly.
4 · Health check
openclaw doctor
Green rows mean go. Anything red, run openclaw doctor --fix first, and if it stays red, that is literally what the purple box at the bottom of this page is for.
5 · Meet your agent
openclaw chat
A terminal chat with your new teammate. First assignment: tell it its name, who you are, and the three things you want help with this month. Which brings us to...
6 · Give it a memory
Gordon's rule, learned the long way: an agent without memory is a goldfish with good manners. Ask yours to keep a memory folder of small files, one fact per file: who your people are, what you are working on, how you like things done. Tell it to read memory before building anything and to save what it learns as it goes. Two months from now this is the difference between a tool and a teammate.
7 · Connect ONE channel, whitelist ONE person
In setup (or later via openclaw configure), connect iMessage or WhatsApp, then pair yourself: 
openclaw pairing
openclaw qr
Your agent should reach exactly one human on day one: you. Expand later, deliberately, per Q3.
8 · Open the dashboard and give it its first real job
openclaw dashboard
Then hand it something real. Suggestions from someone who knows your world: a weekly CYSTEM status digest, a Gayming Awards research binder, or a morning brief that reads your calendar before your shift. Real work, small stakes, immediate payoff.

Questions You Will Have Next Week

Q5How do I keep it safe?
  • Whitelist first, always (Q3). New person = your explicit decision.
  • Drafts before sends for anything professional. Let it write; you hit send.
  • Secrets stay in files with permissions, never pasted into chats: app passwords, API keys, tokens.
  • Weekly checkup: openclaw doctor plus five minutes reading what it did while you were not looking. Trust grows from receipts.
Q6What should it read and never touch?

Read: your calendar, the inboxes you choose, your notes and project files. Never touch without you in the loop: money, anything legal, anything medical, and messages to people going through something tender. The agent handles logistics; you handle love. That division of labor is the entire Darby philosophy.

Q7What does it cost to run?

The Mac Mini you own, electricity you will not notice, OpenClaw is open source, and the Claude subscription or API usage you connect during onboarding. Start with a plan-based setup for predictable cost, watch a month of real usage, then decide if your workload wants more.
